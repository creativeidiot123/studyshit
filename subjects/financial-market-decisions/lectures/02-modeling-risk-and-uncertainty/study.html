Part I · Understand what risk means

Start with everyday risk, then learn when probabilities help and why definitions differ.

Identify risks in everyday decisions

Why do completely different dangerous situations have something in common?

From the slides

Ask for examples outside finance, common characteristics, and a defensible risk definition. These questions frame the later alternatives.

Slides 1, 2, 3 · L001, L002, L003

Distinguish certainty, risk and uncertainty

Can you actually assign reliable probabilities to what might happen?

From the slides

Decisions face outside shocks. Frank Knight's distinction treats risk as stochastically modelable, unlike uncertainty. The slide's normal-density curves illustrate how probabilities can describe different distributions.

Slides 10, 11 · L010, L011

Compare competing definitions of risk

Can a firm be at risk without anything on its balance sheet falling?

From the slides

Asset-based loss misses some cash flows; target deviation is subjective. The lecturer also considers unfavorable future changes and refines that phrase to expected values, cash flows and negative developments.

Slides 17, 18 · L017, L018

Part II · Classify where risk comes from

Use the lecturer's financial-risk maps, then distinguish shocks outside a firm from failures inside it.

Classify business and financial risks

Not all company dangers belong in the same box.

From the slides

Operational, strategic, financial and compliance risks coexist, from near-term processes to longer-term markets. Financial risk further divides into credit, market-price and liquidity branches with named subtypes.

Slides 15, 16 · L015, L016

Separate external from internal risk sources

Who creates the danger: the environment or the organization?

From the slides

External market, strategy and catastrophe shocks differ from internal people, process, IT and legal sources. But reactions to outside risks can feed back into markets, blurring the boundary.

Slides 23, 24 · L023, L024

Part III · See risk in financial decisions

Study what went wrong in lending, review historic failures, and read the Siemens certificate's terms.

Explain the US subprime mortgage crisis

What if the collateral is worth far less right when everyone needs it?

From the slides

The 2007–08 US subprime example links lending incentives, neglected creditworthiness and falling collateral values. NINJA loans, foreclosure and consumer debt spirals illustrate why future repayment capacity matters.

Slides 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 · L004, L005, L006, L007, L008

Recognize landmark financial failures

Why does a hedge fund appear on a slide titled “Bank Failures”?

From the slides

The lecturer lists Danat-Bank (1931), Herstatt (1974), Barings (1995), LTCM (1998), Lehman Brothers (2008), nationalized Hypo Real Estate (2009), and Wirecard (2020). The slide calls these bank failures while explicitly identifying LTCM as a hedge fund.

Slide 9 · L009

Map the Siemens KickStart certificate payoff

What happens to a €182.90 certificate if the share crashes?

From the slides

A Siemens-linked certificate launched in September 2000 has unusual share-delivery, extra-cash and fixed-cash regions at €180 and €220. The lecture invites a payoff graph and gives a severe loss warning; its long-run price chart is adjusted.

Slides 12, 13, 14 · L012, L013, L014

Part IV · Measure risk and judge the models

Choose measures, recognize the models' limits, and finally distinguish market-wide from share-specific risk.

Choose an appropriate risk measure

How much could you lose, and how likely is the bad outcome?

From the slides

Both exposure and event probability matter. Worst case, dispersion/volatility, VaR and probability of missing targets answer different questions. Management must also work when probabilities cannot be quantified.

Slides 19, 22 · L019, L022

Identify three kinds of model risk

A realistic-looking probability can still fail spectacularly.

From the slides

Random extremes, stale parameters and wrong model structure are different failure sources. If many firms act on the same model, risk controls themselves may amplify stress.

Slides 20, 21 · L020, L021

Distinguish systematic from share-specific risk

Can buying 100 different stocks erase a market-wide fall?

From the slides

Company-specific fluctuations are diversifiable; whole-market exposure is not. The lecturer links systematic risk to a premium and introduces beta and CAPM.

Apply the NPV rule

Discount future cash flows, compare investing with distributing cash, and add project NPVs. The lecture survey puts IRR and NPV at the top.

Sources: L002, L003, L004, L005, L006, L007

Calculate and critique the book rate of return

Accounting income relative to book assets is not a cash-flow or market-value measure.

Sources: L008, L030

Compare payback, discounted payback and NPV

A cutoff can reject valuable projects and accept value-destroying ones. Discounting changes which projects recover their investment.

Sources: L009, L010, L011, L012, L030

Calculate IRR and use its decision rule

Solve the NPV equation at zero; compare the resulting rate with the opportunity cost for a conventional investment.

Sources: L013, L014, L015, L016, L017, L030

Distinguish lending from borrowing

Opposite initial cash-flow signs reverse the direction of the NPV profile.

Sources: L018, L030

Detect multiple or missing IRRs

Nonconventional cash flows may cross zero several times or not at all.

Sources: L019, L020, L021, L031

Rank mutually exclusive projects by incremental value

Project scale and cash-flow timing can reverse the IRR ranking; examine incremental expenditure.

Sources: L022, L023, L024, L031

Handle changing opportunity costs of capital

A single IRR comparison becomes problematic with different discount rates by maturity; MIRR is named but not developed in the slides.

Sources: L025, L029, L031

Select projects under capital rationing

Use the lecture's NPV-to-investment ratio and recognize constraints on project combinations.

Sources: L026, L027, L028, L029, L031

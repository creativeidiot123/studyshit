Apply NPV to a project and shareholder value

Would shareholders be richer if the firm spent $1 million on this project?

What the lecturer establishes

Use NPV, not an arbitrary target ratio, to choose value-creating projects.

Slide 1 · L001: Cover: Prof. Dr. Thorsten Feix, Valuing Securities and Companies, IBF Master Lectures first term, Lecture 5-1: Valuing Projects - NPV and Other Investment Criteria.

Slide 2 · L002: Learning goals: understand NPV project evaluation; compare payback period, book rate of return and IRR; evaluate advantages and drawbacks; understand capital rationing and profitability index.

Slide 3 · L003: NPV rule: NPV = C0 + C1/(1+r) + C2/(1+r)^2 + C3/(1+r)^3 + … . C0 is time-zero net cash flow, Ct the date-t cash flow, r the opportunity cost of capital.

Slide 4 · L004: Market value ($ millions): reject X = cash 1 + other assets 9 = 10; accept X = cash 0 + other assets 9 + project PV = 9 + PV. Accepting increases value when PV > 1, equivalently NPV > 0.

Slide 5 · L005: Opportunity cost: shareholders could have invested the same cash in a financial asset. Diagram contrasts manager investing in Project X with dividends or share buybacks and shareholders investing themselves.

Slide 6 · L006: Bar chart labelled usage of appraisal criteria (percent): profitability index 12%; book rate of return 20%; payback 57%; IRR 76%; NPV 75%. Descriptive usage figures, not returns of a given project.

Slide 7 · L007: Three NPV principles: (1) money today is worth more than the same nominal money later; (2) only expected cash flows and opportunity cost of capital determine NPV; (3) present values share today's monetary units and are additive: NPV(A+B)=NPV(A)+NPV(B).

Remember
  • Use NPV, not an arbitrary target ratio, to choose value-creating projects.
  • Watch out: Do not compare future dollars before discounting them.

Recognise the limits of accounting return

A project can look profitable on paper while tying up cash.

What the lecturer establishes

Book rate uses average accounting earnings and average carrying value, not project cash flow.

Slide 8 · L008: Book Rate of Return (Accounting Rate of Return) = average book income over project life / average book assets over project life. Based on earnings and accounting carrying values influenced by depreciation/tax conventions, not cash flows or market values.

Remember
  • Book rate uses average accounting earnings and average carrying value, not project cash flow.
  • Watch out: Accounting policy affects the answer; it is not the market return.

Compare payback with NPV

Would you reject a highly profitable project because its payoff arrives a year late?

What the lecturer establishes

Calculate cumulative undiscounted inflows and compare the payback with a chosen cutoff; then compare with NPV.

Slide 9 · L009: Payback period is years until cumulative cash flows cover initial investment; rule accepts payback at or before a manager-chosen cutoff. Flaws: ignores cash after cutoff and fails to discount flows before cutoff.

Slide 10 · L010: Five-minute class exercise at 10% cost of capital and two-year cutoff. Cash flows $: A [C0=-2,000,C1=500,C2=500,C3=5,000]; B [-2,000,500,1,800,0]; C [-2,000,1,800,500,0]. Compute payback and NPV; discuss financial problems from cutoff.

Slide 11 · L011: Results at r=10%: A payback 3 years and NPV +$2,624 (exact +2,624.34); B payback 2 years and NPV -$58 (exact -57.85); C payback 2 years and NPV +$50 (exact +49.59). Two-year payback rejects best-value A and accepts negative-NPV B.

Remember
  • Calculate cumulative undiscounted inflows and compare the payback with a chosen cutoff; then compare with NPV.
  • Watch out: Payback ignores cash flows after the cutoff and discounts none.

Read the discounted payback table

What changes when you refuse to treat money in year 3 as money today?

What the lecturer establishes

Discount each dated inflow before accumulating it to the initial outlay.

Slide 12 · L012: Discounted payback at 10% uses discounted inflows: A C1 500/1.10=455, C2 500/1.10^2=413, C3 5,000/1.10^3=3,757; discounted payback 3 years; B C1=455,C2=1,488, total below 2,000 so none; C C1=1,636,C2=413, discounted payback 2 years. NPV (rounded) A 2,624, B -58, C 50.

Remember
  • Discount each dated inflow before accumulating it to the initial outlay.
  • Watch out: Discounted payback still ignores value after the recovery date.

Calculate IRR and apply its decision rule

Which discount rate makes the investment break exactly even?

What the lecturer establishes

Solve NPV = 0 for IRR, then compare with the project’s opportunity cost of capital.

Slide 13 · L013: Single cash payoff rate = payoff/investment - 1. The discount rate with NPV = C0 + C1/(1+IRR) = 0 is IRR = C1/(-C0) - 1 for an initial outflow followed by one inflow.

Slide 14 · L014: General IRR definition: solve 0 = C0 + C1/(1+IRR)+C2/(1+IRR)^2+…+CT/(1+IRR)^T. The procedure resembles a bond's yield-to-maturity calculation.

Slide 15 · L015: Tool A costs $4,000 at t0, generates $2,000 at t1 and $4,000 at t2: solve -4,000+2,000/(1+IRR)+4,000/(1+IRR)^2=0, yielding IRR≈28.08% (calculated 28.0776%). Slide gives Excel IRR(A1:C1).

Slide 16 · L016: Conventional Tool A NPV curve: vertical NPV in dollars (about +2,000 to -2,000), horizontal discount rate in percent (0–100); NPV decreases as r rises and crosses zero at about 28%; IRR>r implies NPV>0, IRR<r implies NPV<0 in this case.

Slide 17 · L017: Standard IRR acceptance rule: accept an investment project if its opportunity cost of capital is less than its internal rate of return (for conventional investing cash flows).

Remember
  • Solve NPV = 0 for IRR, then compare with the project’s opportunity cost of capital.
  • Watch out: IRR is a rate, while NPV gives value in money; the simple rule needs a conventional investment.

Distinguish lending from borrowing in the IRR rule

Is a 50% interest rate always good news?

What the lecturer establishes

If money arrives now and you repay later, the project behaves like borrowing rather than investing.

Slide 18 · L018: Pitfall 1 lending vs borrowing: A cash flows [-$1,000,+$1,500], IRR 50%, NPV at 10% +$364 (exact +363.64). B [+ $1,000,-$1,500], IRR 50%, NPV at 10% -$364. A lends, B borrows; lower rates preferable when borrowing. For borrowing-like CFs NPV can rise when the discount rate rises.

Remember
  • If money arrives now and you repay later, the project behaves like borrowing rather than investing.
  • Watch out: For borrowing, a lower rate is better; an automatic IRR-greater-than-cost test misleads.

Recognise multiple IRRs or no IRR

Can the same cash-flow stream break even at two different discount rates?

What the lecturer establishes

Multiple sign changes can produce more than one IRR, or none; inspect NPV at the relevant cost of capital.

Slide 19 · L019: Pitfall 2: Australian-dollar cash flows in billions: C0=-30, C1=+10 through C9=+10, C10=-65; equation NPV=0 has two positive IRRs, approximately 3.50% and 19.54%.

Slide 20 · L020: NPV-vs-discount-rate graph (NPV in A$ billions, rates in %) crosses the zero line twice around 3.50% and 19.54%, reaches positive NPV between those rates, negative outside the interval shown. Slide headline broadly says multiple sign changes 'will' create multiple IRRs, but this is not universal.

Slide 21 · L021: Pitfall 2 no real IRR case: C=[+$1,000,-$3,000,+$2,500]; no IRR for r>-100% (discriminant negative), yet NPV at 10% +$339 (exact +338.84).

Remember
  • Multiple sign changes can produce more than one IRR, or none; inspect NPV at the relevant cost of capital.
  • Watch out: More than one cash-flow sign reversal does not mathematically guarantee multiple real IRRs.
Fact check (slide 20): Multiple cash-flow sign changes can permit multiple IRRs, but do not guarantee them. Slide 31 correctly says several or no IRRs.

Choose the higher-value mutually exclusive project

Would you prefer a 100% return on a small project to a larger euro gain?

What the lecturer establishes

Compare project NPVs and, where useful, compute incremental cash flows when scale and timing differ.

Slide 22 · L022: Pitfall 3, mutually exclusive alternatives at r=10%: D [-$10,000,+$20,000] has IRR 100%, NPV +$8,182; E [-$20,000,+$35,000] has IRR 75%, NPV +$11,818. E has lower IRR but higher NPV, so E is preferable if only one project can be taken.

Slide 23 · L023: Incremental E−D: [-$10,000,+$15,000] yields IRR 50% and NPV +$3,636 at 10%. Timing example: F C0=-9,000,C1=6,000,C2=5,000,C3=4,000,C4=0,C5=0,…; IRR≈33%, NPV=3,592. G C0=-9,000,C1=C2=C3=C4=C5=1,800,… as continuing stream; IRR≈20%, NPV=9,000. Difference G−F: C0=0,C1=-4,200,C2=-3,200,C3=-2,200,C4=C5=1,800,…; slide IRR≈15.6%, incremental NPV=5,408 at 10%.

Slide 24 · L024: F/G NPV graph: x-axis discount rate % (~0–50), y-axis NPV $ (about -5,000 to +10,000); F reaches NPV=0 near IRR 33.3%, G near IRR 15.6% per red curve label, while alternative table on slide 23 reports G IRR 20% and G−F IRR 15.6%. Treat the plotted 15.6% label and table separately; curve labels likely represent G−F incremental rate rather than G's own IRR. At 10%, G's NPV exceeds F's.

Remember
  • Compare project NPVs and, where useful, compute incremental cash flows when scale and timing differ.
  • Watch out: Ranking mutually exclusive alternatives by their individual IRRs can destroy value.
Fact check (slide 24): The graph labels G at 15.6%, whereas the slide-23 table lists G at about 20% and G−F at 15.6%. Do not silently identify these values as the same IRR.

Handle changing opportunity costs of capital

What if year 1 and year 5 require different discount rates?

What the lecturer establishes

A single IRR cannot be compared cleanly against several period-specific opportunity costs.

Slide 25 · L025: Pitfall 4: different opportunity costs of capital for early and late cash flows (term structure). Slide presents constant-r NPV equation and claims IRR assumes stable rates, 'implies all funds reinvested at IRR', and labels that assumption false. Fact check: plain IRR computation itself does not require intermediate reinvestment at IRR; comparing one IRR with a full term structure can nonetheless be misleading.

Remember
  • A single IRR cannot be compared cleanly against several period-specific opportunity costs.
  • Watch out: The slide states an IRR reinvestment assumption; distinguish that claim from the IRR calculation itself.
Fact check (slide 25): The slide describes IRR as assuming reinvestment at IRR. Computing the root of NPV=0 does not itself impose any actual reinvestment plan; varying costs of capital remain a valid limitation of comparing a single IRR.

Use profitability index under capital constraints

With too little cash to fund every positive-NPV proposal, what earns more per dollar committed?

What the lecturer establishes

The lecturer’s PI is NPV divided by investment; rank value per unit of scarce capital.

Slide 26 · L026: Profitability index on slide defined PI=NPV/investment (distinct from alternative textbook convention 1+NPV/outlay). Cash flows in $m and NPV at r=10%: A [-10,+30,+5], NPV≈21, PI≈2.1; B [-5,+5,+20], NPV≈16, PI≈3.2; C [-5,+5,+15], NPV≈12, PI≈2.4; D [0,-40,+60], NPV≈13, PI≈0.4 when t1 investment cost is discounted. Exact NPVs 21.405,16.074,11.942,13.223.

Slide 27 · L027: PI used for selecting project combinations under scarce capital. Slide asserts the highest weighted-average PI identifies an optimal project set; qualification: simple PI ordering is reliable only under restrictive assumptions (e.g. divisible investments and one binding capital constraint), not arbitrary indivisible combinations.

Remember
  • The lecturer’s PI is NPV divided by investment; rank value per unit of scarce capital.
  • Watch out: Simple PI rankings can fail with indivisible projects or multiple constraints.
Fact check (slide 27): A simple PI ranking need not produce the optimal bundle with indivisible projects or multiple constraints. Slide 31 also warns about constrained investment combinations.

Tell soft from hard capital rationing

Why might a profitable project go unfunded?

What the lecturer establishes

Capital rationing limits available investment funds, imposed internally or by capital-market availability.

Slide 28 · L028: Capital rationing = cap on funds available for investment. Soft rationing is management-imposed; hard rationing stems from inability to obtain funds in capital markets.

Remember
  • Capital rationing limits available investment funds, imposed internally or by capital-market availability.
  • Watch out: A budget cap does not make NPV irrelevant.

Consolidate the investment rules

Which rule would you trust in the exam when different criteria disagree?

What the lecturer establishes

Review the terms, four IRR pitfalls, constrained-budget caveats and assigned reading.

Slide 29 · L029: Key terms listed: Payback Period; Discounted Payback Rule; Book Rate of Return; Internal Rate of Return; mutually exclusive projects; Modified Internal Rate of Return (MIRR, named only, not defined); capital rationing with soft/hard types; profitability index.

Slide 30 · L030: Summary I: accounting book rate depends on depreciation/book asset treatment, not cash flows or time value; payback cutoff is ad hoc and ignores timing/post-cutoff flows; IRR zeroes NPV and normally is accepted if above cost of capital; first trap: borrowing-type flows can show NPV rising with discount rate.

Slide 31 · L031: Summary II: multiple CF sign changes may cause multiple or no IRRs; exclusive projects can differ in life/scale and need incremental comparison; costs of capital may vary across years; one-period rationing uses PI ranking, which can fail for multi-period constraints or restrictions; lecturer names linear programming as general solution.

Slide 32 · L032: Required: Brealey, Myers & Allen (2023), Principles of Corporate Finance, International Student Edition, 14th ed., Chapter 5 'NPV and other investment criteria', pp.119–148. Supplementary: Pettit, Strategic Corporate Finance; Copeland, Weston & Shastri (2005), Financial Theory and Corporate Policy (4th ed.); Benninga (2008), Financial Modeling (3rd ed.).

Remember
  • Review the terms, four IRR pitfalls, constrained-budget caveats and assigned reading.
  • Watch out: The key-term list names modified IRR but gives no definition or formula on these slides.

Part 1: Read the equity market

Start with market information, the way stocks trade, and the financial inputs used to compare them.

Read market indices and sectors

The S&P 500 fell 25.61 points. Is that a crash, or barely a wobble?

Start wide, then zoom in

To assess corporate performance, the lecture first opens the Financial Times market dashboard. An equity index tracks a group of stocks, so it summarizes a market rather than the price of one company. Choose a region, then compare indices and sectors before opening an individual stock. The dashboard offers equities, currencies, commodities, bonds, funds and ETFs, plus watchlists.

Read the latest level, today's change, 52-week range and session separately. The five-day FTSE 100 line rises 1.63% even though its daily quote falls 0.14%; a different time window can tell a different story.

Translate points into a percentage

Use a percentage change when the question compares movements of indices with different starting levels. Use point changes only for movement in the same index units; neither is automatically an investor's total return.

Previous level = current level − signed point change.
Daily change (%) = (signed point change ÷ previous level) × 100.

Both levels and the signed change are in index points; the resulting percentage is dimensionless, for the same session relative to the previous close. Previous level must be positive.

Worked example · S&P 500 (L005–L006)
  1. GIVEN: last level 6,370.17 points; today's signed change −25.61 points, historical 22 Aug 2025 snapshot.
  2. Same index and daily period; do not use the one-year change or the 52-week extremes.
  3. Previous close = 6,370.17 − (−25.61) = 6,395.78 points.
  4. Daily change = (−25.61 ÷ 6,395.78) × 100 = -0.40042%.
  5. Rounded: −0.40%, matching the slide.
  6. Sanity check: 25.61 is small relative to roughly 6,400; negative direction and less than 1% magnitude make sense.

Your turn: Hang Seng rose 211.43 points to 25,316.04. Calculate the daily percentage change to two decimals.

Hint and worked answer

Previous close = 25,316.04 − 211.43 = 25,104.61 points. 211.43 ÷ 25,104.61 × 100 = 0.84220%, rounded to +0.84%.

Inspect the chart and sector breakdown

On the index detail page, price history answers how the level moved, while volume bars indicate trading activity. The S&P's April dip and recovery sit inside the 52-week range; the one-year change is +13.81%. A sector groups companies by business area. The lecture's sectors include consumer staples, basic materials, telecommunications, industrials, financials, health care, real estate, technology, consumer discretionary and utilities. Different sectors need not move together. Read the count columns and sparkline horizon cautiously when headings are cropped.

Quick check: Which metric best compares the S&P 500's and Hang Seng's daily movements?

Answer and why

Daily percentage change. It normalizes point changes by the previous close, making movements comparable.

L021 · Slide 21 · Intrinsic dividend valuation

Valuation principle: value of a stock = PV of future (free) cash flows (to the firm), then equity approach PV(stock)=PV(expected future dividends per share). P₀=Σ[t=1…∞] Divₜ/(1+r)ᵗ. P₀ share price today; Divₜ expected dividend per share in future period t; r opportunity cost, expected return, cost of capital/discount rate, equity approach cost of equity. Slide concludes share value or firm value equals discounted dividends (per share). Flag: per-share dividends discounted at cost of equity yield equity value per share, not enterprise value.

Visual annotation targets: Equity approach arrow, summation and symbol labels.

L022 · Slide 22 · Holding-period return

Expected return (market capitalization rate, total shareholder return) is forecast percentage yield over a specific period: r=(Div₁+P₁−P₀)/P₀. Dividend plus capital gain; prices/dividend in currency per share, r per holding period.

L034 · Slide 34 · Gordon growth model

L039 · Slide 39 · Payout and retention

L061 · Slide 61 · Enterprise DCF formula

Enterprise valuation usually discounts FCF to valuation horizon H plus terminal value. FCF is after-tax operating cash flow after investment in growth (working capital, PP&E, etc.). Lecture names WACC in objectives/key terms but does not give WACC formula. For operating FCF the consistent enterprise discount rate is WACC (clarification for later teaching).

L066 · Slide 66 · Summary: PV equilibrium

Common stock value equals indefinite stream of dividend cash payments discounted at expected return on other securities with equivalent risk: PV=Σ[t=1…∞] Divₜ/(1+r)ᵗ. Starts from short investment horizons with dividends and capital gains, not a premise that investors only want dividends. P₀=(DIV₁+P₁)/(1+r) is equilibrium condition; if price over/under fair value, selling/buying pushes price to adjust.

Visual annotation targets: Two summary formula boxes and equilibrium mechanism.

L067 · Slide 67 · Summary: growth and business

Growing perpetuity P₀=DIV₁/(r−g); r=DIV₁/P₀+g, g=plowback ratio×ROE. P₀=EPS₁/r+PVGO, where EPS₁/r is PV of no-growth earnings/share and PVGO is NPV of investments to grow. Same discounted cash-flow principle extends to entire businesses using FCF instead of dividend/share.

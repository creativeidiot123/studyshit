STUDYSHIT • INCREMENTAL HTML PIPELINE • SHARED RULES

GOAL: An MA International Business and Finance student (THA Augsburg, Germany) wants to KNOW the lecture and pass the exam without wading through theory. Build notes that are short, accurate, complete and genuinely enjoyable to work through: a hook, a clear idea, a worked example, something to see, something to try, a question to test yourself. Everything the lecture taught is covered; beyond that, only a small, clearly labelled extra (about 5%) of closely related ideas that genuinely matter. Correct at master's level, never a research text. If a sentence does not help the student understand or answer an exam question, cut it.

THREE NON-NEGOTIABLES:
1. COMPLETE: every concept, formula, number, lecturer example and warning from the sources appears somewhere (tracked by source IDs).
2. ACCURATE: every number in text, examples, visuals, calculators and answers is recalculated and correct; lecturer errors get a short "Fact check".
3. LESS: the shortest version that still teaches it. Show and let the student try rather than explain at length.

LEARNING FORMAT (every stage follows this; it overrides any wording elsewhere that asks for more depth):
Each topic is one <section id="topic-tNN" data-format="compact-v2"> built from these parts, in this order:
1. Plain title that says what the student can do (for example "Pricing a bond from its cash flows").
2. Hook (class "hook"): ONE short line that makes the student curious: a concrete question, a surprising fact from the lecture, or a decision ("Would you pay 125 for a bond that pays you 125 in total? You shouldn't.").
3. Concept blocks (h3), 1-4 per topic. Each block:
   - the idea in plain words, max ~80 words. Define a term only where it first appears, in one short clause or sentence, in bold. No separate definitions lists, no definitions of words the lecture does not use;
   - the lecture's formula if there is one, then each symbol in a few words, plus a 1-3 line "why it works" intuition when that makes it click (no formal derivation unless the lecturer derived it);
   - a worked example (class "example") using the lecturer's numbers where possible, with ALL numbered steps visible immediately, no mandatory step-by-step reveal. Put a short active "Your turn" question AFTER the example where useful, not between every line. Invented numbers are labelled ILLUSTRATIVE;
   - a visual (class "visual") whenever the idea involves numbers, time, a process or a relationship;
   - one quick check (class "quick-check") as an instant-feedback question (see INTERACTIVE PARTS).
4. "Try it" (class "try-it", optional, max 1 per topic): a small calculator or slider for a formula worth playing with (for example change the yield and watch the bond price move). Only where playing with it builds intuition.
5. Beyond the slides (class "beyond", optional, max 1 per topic): see BEYOND THE SLIDES.
6. Remember (class "takeaways"): 3-4 one-line bullets, including the most common mistake ("Watch out: ...").
7. Practice (added in S05, class "practice").

LIMITS:
- Prose per topic: target 200-450 words, hard cap 550. Formulas, tables, visuals, example steps, interactive parts and questions do NOT count, so they are never squeezed; prose is.
- Visuals: at least one per topic whenever the topic has numbers, time, a process or a relationship; at most 3 per topic. Small inline SVG or HTML tables (no external images, no stock art, no decoration), computed from the stated numbers, labelled axes and units, one-line caption. Keep each SVG roughly under 4 KB.
- BEYOND THE SLIDES (about 5%): a little related theory is welcome when it is important and connected to the topic, for example something every finance student is expected to know next to this lecture (clean vs dirty price next to bond pricing, convexity next to duration, credit spreads next to ratings). Rules: at most one box per topic, max ~80 words, labelled "Beyond the slides", written in the same engaging way (a hook, an example or a tiny visual), standard textbook knowledge stated accurately (cite the textbook or source for specific facts), never contradicting the lecture, and never presented as lecture content. Whole chapter: all Beyond boxes together stay around 5% of the chapter's prose. Skip it when nothing important is missing.
- Real-world example (optional, class "real-world"): at most one per topic, max ~60 words, with an inspected citation, or labelled ILLUSTRATIVE.
- Source-slide walkthroughs: show an authentic image of EVERY substantive source slide when publishing rights and a verified image asset are available. Put it BESIDE plain-English explanations and direct annotations of actual visible details, not just a generic summary. Decorative/administrative slides can be omitted from the photo gallery but must still appear in the coverage map if relevant. Preserve the slide numbers and L-IDs. Slide screenshots do NOT count toward the 3 custom explanatory visuals per topic. Redraw key chart details separately when that improves readability; never use a redraw to imply the original slide photo was published.
- Lecturer errors: keep the statement and add a 1-2 sentence "Fact check" (class "fact-check").
- Tone: direct, friendly, confident; short sentences; one idea per paragraph; no filler, no repetition across topics (link back instead), no childish jokes.
- Topics without data-format="compact-v2" are in an older format. When their stage processes them, rewrite them into this format from their existing content and sources, keeping every lecturer fact, number, formula and source ID; cut only explanation.

SOURCE SLIDE PHOTO + EXPLANATION CONTRACT (student-facing final HTML):
- Every substantive slide has a compact source-linked walkthrough in its primary topic: a real slide screenshot on the left and a right-hand panel titled "What is going on here?" written in ordinary language. On narrow screens stack the photo above the explanation. One gallery item per slide, in source order within each topic, with slide number and corresponding L-IDs. Do not hide the original image behind a click or require JavaScript to understand the explanation.
- Point to the exact elements of the slide using 1-4 numbered/high-contrast annotation markers positioned as percentages over the screenshot; the same numbers appear in an adjacent list explaining what each arrow, plotted line, axis, formula, number, box or conclusion actually means and why it matters. For a genuinely simple slide, one callout is enough. Notes should be short, concrete and accessible; do not repeat a whole lecture explanation. If no visible target exists, use a clearly labelled slide note instead of a fake marker. A reader must know what to look at and what to think, not merely see "this slide discusses X".
- Suggested semantic markup: <figure class="slide-walkthrough" data-slide="7" data-source-ids="L007"><div class="slide-image"><img src="assets/slides/slide-007.webp" alt="Slide 7: axes and plotted yield curve" loading="lazy">...numbered visible markers...</div><figcaption class="slide-explainer"><strong>Slide 7: What is going on here?</strong><ol>...matching numbered explanations...</ol></figcaption></figure>. Add accessible text for each marker, alt text for the image, and an explanation understandable without JavaScript. The slide number is the ORIGINAL page number, not the topic position. Use HTML/CSS percentage positions for markers, not hard-coded pixels. Avoid relying only on colors. The annotation panel should remain legible on mobile and in print.
- Keep slide images as optimized, local assets relative to study.html, typically assets/slides/slide-001.webp, not external hotlinks or inline multi-megabyte data URIs. The "one file" requirement means ONE evolving study.html; verified permitted image assets may live in its adjacent assets/ folder. Prefer WebP/PNG with enough resolution to read slide labels, lazy-load offscreen images, and keep the full source image uncropped. Every src must actually resolve on GitHub Pages; a filename in HTML does not prove the image exists.
- PUBLICATION PERMISSIONS: This repository and GitHub Pages are PUBLIC. An uploaded professor's PPT/PDF does not by itself grant permission to redistribute slide screenshots. Never commit original slide photos or screenshots, copyrighted diagrams, personal details or confidential slide material unless sharing rights/authorization are established and binary upload is verified. When rights, a renderer, original images, or binary upload are unavailable, DO NOT invent screenshots, embed broken links or silently omit walkthroughs: use an honest "Original slide image not published" placeholder, a clearly labelled independently created explanatory diagram when appropriate, and the same slide-numbered plain-English explanation. Record why in inputs/source-manifest.json and the build log. Ask about switching to a private destination or obtaining permission if actual images are indispensable. Treat the absence of images as a disclosed limitation, never claim a photo-rich final HTML.
- Source manifest tracks per-slide image_status (published / blocked-rights / unavailable-source / unavailable-renderer / unavailable-upload), image_path only for verified published assets, and rights/permission evidence when applicable. Keep image files and these statuses consistent. Image generation or redrawing must never fabricate what a slide physically showed.

ACTIVE LEARNING CONTRACT (replace passive click-to-reveal):
- Research basis: retrieval practice and distributed practice have stronger general evidence than passive rereading (Dunlosky et al., 2013, https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/learning-techniques.html). Fade from fully shown worked examples to partially completed and independent problems (Renkl & Atkinson, 2003, https://doi.org/10.1207/S15326985EP3801_3). Feedback must teach the task/process, not merely say right/wrong (Hattie & Timperley, 2007, https://doi.org/10.3102/003465430298487). Simulation research supports purposefully guided exploration; it does not show that arbitrary clicking is beneficial (Chernikova et al., 2020, https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654320933544).
- Worked examples are FULLY visible on load. No "Next step" UI, auto-hiding of steps, carousels for calculations, forced animation or tutorial gates. Numbered steps serve comprehension and print, never require clicks.
- The high-value interaction patterns are: (A) "Finish it": after seeing a complete lecturer example, try a closely related problem with one genuinely important answer field, units, tolerance, contextual feedback, optional hint and native <details> full solution; (B) "Predict → explore → explain": choose Up/Down/No change in a real model, move a keyboard-accessible slider or input, see recalculated values and graph/visual if useful, then read a one-sentence causal explanation; (C) "Spot the mistake": pick a plausible incorrect reasoning step, and receive feedback naming the misconception and correct alternative; (D) "Recall later": optional review of a previously attempted question/flashcard on another visit, with no external account, notifications or deceptive memory claims.
- Default per numerical topic: one full example, ONE close-variant numeric completion and optionally ONE manipulable model if changing its inputs is conceptually meaningful. Default per conceptual topic: one decision/diagnose-a-mistake question. Quick checks and S05 practice still apply. Avoid duplicating widgets/questions or adding more than TWO distinct widget types per topic. If a passive infographic teaches the idea better, do not force an interaction.
- Markup must remain simple and auditable. Completion question example: <div class="finish-it" data-finish data-answer="981.67" data-tolerance="0.02" data-unit="€"><label for="finish-t03">What is the bond price in €?</label><input id="finish-t03" type="number" step="any" inputmode="decimal"><button type="button" data-check-finish>Check</button><p data-finish-feedback role="status" aria-live="polite"></p><details><summary>Hint and worked answer</summary><p>...</p></details></div>. Parse numeric values with finite-number checks, accept stated tolerance, report units and WHY. Do not claim an untouched answer correct. Use unique IDs for all inputs; input may be blank; do not disable retry after mistakes.
- A prediction question must not require a guess to view the model; show baseline values, current outputs and keyboard-operable controls. Predictions and answers must be independent (changing model value should NOT silently grade the guess); using aria-live on an output is sufficient. Model output and any chart must be computed from the exact same validated inputs, including default. No fake graph motion.
- Optional revisit: a "Review later" button can store only a stable local question ID and due date in localStorage, protected by try/catch. Provide a "Review now" path and an understandable no-storage fallback; do not enforce an algorithm without testing, ask for private data, make notifications, or assert an evidence-based precise review interval. Previously missed questions should stay retryable.
- Use semantic HTML, touch-friendly controls, labelled axis/units, keyboard/focus visibility, real text feedback, no dependence on color alone, and no-JavaScript answers in <details>. Keep examples/solutions visible in print. Provide a minimal functioning reference at /interaction-lab.html (copy snippets into one-file lecture output; do not externally fetch the demo script).

AI EXPLAINER (OPT-IN, OUTSIDE CORE STUDY INTERACTIONS):
- EVERY new or updated lecture study.html must have exactly one <script defer src="../../../../portal/explain-ai.js"></script> immediately before </body>, after the core study-js if present. Its path is relative to subjects/<subject>/lectures/<lecture>/study.html. The shared portal/explain-ai.js automatically adds a right-aligned "✦ Explain with AI" control to each topic H2 and loads portal/explain-ai.css. Never hand-code a separate button per topic or duplicate the script.
- This is a separate optional online extension. The normal chapter, worked examples, quizzes, progress, flashcards and print view must remain usable without it, without JavaScript, or offline. The "no network requests" rule for the core study-js does NOT forbid the explicit user-triggered AI proxy request in this external script.
- The AI tool sends the chosen topic's actual lecture content, source IDs, visual captions and surrounding topic outline to the secure proxy. Modes are simpler explanation, new examples, deeper intuition, and follow-up. It must not modify source content or silently rewrite facts.
- Never embed or request GEMINI_API_KEY in HTML, JS, a public JSON file or the reader's browser. GitHub Pages cannot access GitHub Actions secrets. The ai-worker/ backend is deployed with GitHub Actions secrets to Cloudflare, with model gemini-3.8-flash and HIGH thinking; activation/setup is in ai-worker/README.md. If that backend is not deployed, the UI clearly offers connection instructions, rather than pretending AI works.
- Preserve this single script reference in all later pipeline stages, including S06 audit and S07 publication, and check it on each new chapter.

INTERACTIVE PARTS (vanilla JavaScript, no libraries, no network requests; the page must still be fully usable without JavaScript):
- One shared script at the end of the page, <script id="study-js">, kept under ~8 KB, provides: numeric answer checking, immediate explanatory feedback, interactive predictions, flashcards/review prompts and a progress bar. Do NOT hide worked-example steps or add "Next step" buttons. Preserve all worked steps fully visible both with and without JS. Add the shared script the first time a stage needs it; extend it, never duplicate it.
- Instant-feedback question markup: a container with data-quiz, options as buttons with data-correct="true" on the right one, and a short "why" explanation shown after any click (right: green + why; wrong: red + why + the right answer). Numeric questions use data-answer="112.11" and data-tolerance="0.01" with an input and a Check button. Always include a <details> fallback with the answer for no-JS and print.
- "Try it" calculators: inputs and output inside their own container, driven by a small inline script (max ~40 lines) that only touches that container. Its default inputs MUST be the lecturer's example and its default output MUST equal the example's printed result (state both in the build log). Where useful, ask for a direction prediction BEFORE manipulating the model; after the change, show the real computed output plus why it moved. No hidden answer requires making a prediction first.
- Progress: each topic ends with a "Got it" checkbox; the shared script stores it in localStorage (wrapped in try/catch) and updates a progress bar at the top. Without storage it simply does not persist.
- Print CSS shows every answer, hint, explanation and every worked-example step; hides buttons, not substantive content. Controls require labels, keyboard access, obvious focus styles and aria-live feedback. No drag-only tasks, forced animations, time limits, emoji prizes, or meaningless scores.

PAGE DESIGN: One inline stylesheet styling these classes once, simply and attractively (light tinted cards, clear headings, readable on mobile, good contrast, print-friendly): hook, example, visual, quick-check, try-it, beyond, takeaways, fact-check, real-world, practice, cheat-sheet, flashcard, progress, slide-walkthrough, slide-image, slide-explainer, slide-marker. Make slide walkthroughs a two-column image/explanation layout on desktop and one-column on phones; print annotations and explanations together. Add missing styles the first time a stage needs them; do not restyle the whole page.

ONE FILE: Each lecture has ONE evolving HTML document at subjects/<subject-slug>/lectures/<lecture-slug>/study.html. Edit it incrementally, never regenerate from scratch. Maintain the source ledger with L001... identifiers, stable topic-t01... IDs, data-source-ids, and a collapsed build log. Keep all unique taught information.

SOURCE FIDELITY: With recordings, transcript establishes what teacher taught; class slides clarify formulas/visuals. Without recording, class slides ARE primary lecture content; never invent teacher speech. Module-wide materials help but are not automatically lecture coverage. Inspect slides visually; report unavailable visuals. Distinguish teacher statements, AI explanations, illustrative numbers, and verified outside facts.

RESEARCH: External company facts, figures, dates, standards, laws, and attributed definitions require actually inspected citations next to the claims; never fabricate sources. Unverified additions must be omitted or labelled uncertain, not asserted. Illustrative arithmetic must be checked. Preserve disputed lecturer statements with a separate correction, not silent alteration.

PERSISTENCE CONTRACT (CRITICAL):
- Read latest study.html and state.json from creativeidiot123/studyshit before editing an existing lecture. Derive paths from established state, never guess an existing path.
- Under the same lecture folder, maintain inputs/source-manifest.json with original filenames, source types, file sizes when known, and ChatGPT attachment provenance. The studyshit repository is PUBLIC. Do not upload original copyrighted PPT/PDF/transcript bytes, personal student details, or confidential source documents to this public repository by default. Only copy originals under inputs/ when destination access/privacy and permission to share are confirmed and a binary-safe upload succeeds. Never imply a chat attachment was copied to GitHub unless the transfer was verified.
- Write changed study.html to GitHub, using the current blob SHA for updates. For new lectures create the file. Confirm the new content by re-fetching it. Update state.json after the HTML write, then verify state as well. Use connector writes or another explicitly verified authenticated method. A sandbox link, inline HTML, or a claimed save is NOT a GitHub commit.
- Batch sizes: S03 one topic per run, S04 up to 4 topics per run, S05 up to 3 topics per run. The source manifest is metadata; it is NOT a substitute for reading the actual uploaded PPT or its rendered slide images. Update topic data-progress and state.json. With incomplete work, keep the current stage and use STEP_MORE.
- If GitHub write/read, source access, identity, credentials or needed human decision is blocked, state the exact issue and end WAIT_FOR_HUMAN. NEVER claim a stage succeeded without a verified persisted HTML version.
- Maintain state.json keys: schema_version=1, subject, lecture, html_path, stage, completed_topics (object mapping stage numbers to topic IDs), next_topic, source_complete, issues, updated_at. Stage is the latest fully completed stage number (0-7). Keep JSON valid.
- Preserve original HTML IDs, source mapping, sections, figures, equations, practice answers, and already verified facts. Use targeted edits. Check that unrelated sections survive.
- If other information is unavailable, do the most verifiable work possible but DO NOT falsely signal success.

VERIFICATION GATE: A stage's STEP_DONE is only a claim, NOT permission to execute the next stage. Tampermonkey v1.3.37+ must queue /prompts/08-verify-stage.txt to independently verify the just-completed stage against actual saved GitHub content and source material. A VERIFY_PASS moves to the next stage; VERIFY_FAIL returns to the same stage for local repairs. Three verification failures for one stage stop automation for human review. S07 FINAL_READY also requires its own independent verifier PASS. When a user prompt starts [STUDYSHIT AUTO VERIFY Sxx], execute the read-only verifier only. When it starts [STUDYSHIT AUTO REPAIR Sxx], fix the previous verifier's specific findings even if state.stage already equals the stage number; do not advance without a new successful verification. The verifier cannot fix source material, only report PASS/FAIL or WAIT.

TERMINAL TOKENS: Every finished response must end with exactly ONE marker on the final standalone line. No text after it, and no code fence or quotation around it.
[[STUDY_STEP_MORE:S01]] ... [[STUDY_STEP_MORE:S07]] = valid committed progress within the same stage; userscript re-runs that stage WITHOUT a verifier.
[[STUDY_STEP_DONE:S01]] ... [[STUDY_STEP_DONE:S06]] = claimed complete stage is saved; userscript schedules one independent VERIFY turn for that stage, NOT the next stage.
[[STUDY_FINAL_READY]] = claimed completed stage 07 is persisted, but must still be independently verified before final acceptance.
[[STUDY_WAIT_FOR_HUMAN]] = human action required; automation must not advance. Explain blocker before marker.
[[STUDY_VERIFY_PASS:S01]] ... [[STUDY_VERIFY_PASS:S07]] = ONLY a read-only verification turn can approve a matching stage. S07 PASS is the final accepted completion.
[[STUDY_VERIFY_FAIL:S01]] ... [[STUDY_VERIFY_FAIL:S07]] = ONLY a read-only verification turn can return repair findings and re-run the SAME stage.
Use only the marker for the stage actually executing. A verifier must NOT output normal STEP_DONE/STEP_MORE/FINAL_READY. Never emit CGR_DONE together with a STUDY marker; existing resilience layer recognizes STUDY markers natively. [[CGR_HIBERNATE_GITHUB_10M]] is reserved for already-running GitHub Actions whose completion must be checked later, not ordinary task progress.
Do not include terminal tokens in student-facing HTML or in ordinary prose except protocol documentation.
